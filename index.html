Hello, I’m Madison

I started Hazel after planning my own wedding and realizing the part I loved most was the part everyone else dreaded.

Now I take on a limited number of Utah weddings each year and stay with each one from the first budget conversation to the last vendor payment. I build the timeline, hold the vendor relationships, and stand at the back of the room on the day making sure nothing needs you. You show up, get married, and spend the evening with your people.

Where I work

Based in Salt Lake City, planning weddings across Utah

Hazel is based in Salt Lake City. Every collection covers weddings within 60 miles of the city, and I’m happy to go further: travel beyond that is quoted separately and up front, never folded into a surprise invoice.

A lot of my couples don’t live here. If you’re getting married in Utah but planning from another state, I become your eyes on the ground, with venue walkthroughs, tastings and mock-ups, all reported back with photos and notes.

Why hire a planner

Someone should be watching the clock. It shouldn’t be you.

A wedding is a few hundred small decisions and about forty people who all need to be in the right place at the right time. My job is to have made the decisions with you months ago, so that on the day there is nothing left to decide.

  • i.A timeline every vendor has seen. Built weeks out, shared with everyone, and protected on the day. Nobody asks you what happens next.
  • ii.One person to call. The florist calls me. The caterer texts me. Your mother finds me. You get to be a guest at your own wedding.
  • iii.Someone who has read every contract. Overtime clauses, rain plans, load-in windows. I catch the things that turn into surprises later.
  • iv.A calm person at the back of the room. If a thing goes wrong, and one small thing always does, you will hear about it after the honeymoon, if at all.

Utah planning FAQ

Questions about planning a wedding in Utah

Where in Utah do you plan weddings?
I’m based in Salt Lake City, and every collection includes weddings within 60 miles of the city. Weddings further away, in Utah or out of state, are welcome and quoted separately for travel.
What’s the difference between a wedding planner and a wedding coordinator?
A planner helps you make the decisions over months: budget, vendors, design and logistics. A coordinator carries out decisions you’ve already made, starting about six weeks before the wedding and running through the day. My Partial and Full-Service collections include both. Month-of coordination is only the second half.
How much does a wedding planner cost in Utah?
It depends on how much you hand over. Coordination costs the least, and full-service costs the most because it covers the whole engagement. My current prices are on the planning page, and I wrote a plain-English guide to wedding planner costs if you’re comparing options.
How far ahead should we book a Utah wedding planner?
Popular Utah dates—Saturdays from May through October—tend to book 10 to 14 months out for full-service and partial planning. Month-of coordination can be booked as late as four months before the wedding. If your date is sooner, ask anyway.
