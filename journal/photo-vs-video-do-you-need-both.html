Photo, video, or both? An honest answer

August 5, 20265 min readBy Hazel Wedding Co.

We sell both, so take this with the grain of salt it deserves. But we also get asked this constantly, and the honest answer isn’t “both, obviously.” It’s: they do different jobs, and one of them is much more often regretted when skipped.

What photos are for

Photos are for the wall, the album, the phone lock screen, the grandparents. They’re the thing you’ll look at most, by a wide margin. A great photograph is a still moment you can live inside for a long time. If you can only afford one, this is the one. Every couple we’ve ever talked to agrees.

What film is for

Film is for the things a photograph can’t hold: the sound of your partner’s voice saying the vows, your dad’s toast, the way your grandmother laughed. Couples who skip video rarely miss the visuals. They miss the audio. The most common thing we hear, years later, is “I wish I could hear it again.”

Nobody regrets the photos. The people who regret skipping video regret the sound.

Where the money does the most good

If your budget is tight, our honest ranking:

  1. A great photographer for the core of the day. Six to eight hours, one shooter. This is non-negotiable.
  2. A short film with real audio. Even a highlight film that captures the vows and toasts is worth more than an extra two hours of photo coverage.
  3. Then second shooters, longer coverage, full ceremony edits, drone.

The case for the same team

If you do book both, book them from the same studio if you can. Photographers and videographers who don’t know each other compete for the same angle at the same moment, and the couple ends up with a lens in every frame. Teams that work together split the room without talking. It also means one timeline, one contract and one person to text. We built our whole studio around that idea, and our photo + film collections are priced to make it the easy choice.

If you still have to choose

Photos. Then, if there’s anything left, a friend with a phone standing very still during the vows and the toasts, recording audio. It won’t be cinema. But you’ll be able to hear it again.
